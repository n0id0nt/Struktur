# Seven Gods RPG — Revised Plot Outline

## What changed, and why

This outline goes back to the original design (the Narrative RPG Game idea doc in Google Drive) and builds the new story on top of it. Where the two disagree, the original wins unless this new brief changes it.

## Kept from the original design

- Six gods, each ruling a region whose people are kept in a strict hierarchy. Their power comes from the world itself, and they keep it through control and subjugation, handing the most powerful elemental items to the officials who enforce their rule. A hidden seventh god whose people lived without fear of their rulers, and whose land drew so many people away that the six banded together to destroy him.

- The player fuses with a soul fragment that has no memory. Each fragment brings back a piece of the past.

- The gods were once mortals who grew so powerful there was no difference.

- The old blacksmith is the hidden master and the last follower of the seventh god. He reveals himself at the end and fights in the last arc.

- The cast: the hunter and his gullible daughter, the bandit leader with the necklace, the slum kid who wants to be a knight, the witch on the run with an orphan, the gossiping merchant who knows everything, the sea captain, and the general and his runaway son.

- The tutorial beats: the blacksmith's fetch quest, the hunter's training, the trap that separates you, the fusion, the boss room, and the hand-in.

## Removed from the previous version

- The player slowly becoming a tyrant, Benevolence Points, NPC dependency, and the "corrupted allies" boss arc.

- Companions who become regional controllers. Each companion now has a personal subplot that ends well because of the player, not a corruption arc.

- The Youth Academy and Trade Routes regions. The merchant travels everywhere instead of owning a region.

## New in this version

- Character first. Every region is built around one companion's personal problem. The player helps solve it, and the companion helps beat that region's boss.

- The past is the hook. Each fragment plays a memory cutscene, and every region teaches one more truth about how the world was ruined.

- The head god is renamed to something less on-the-nose, along with the other six. The last twist: the world's ruin was caused by the head god himself, hoarding the world's life to fuel his revenge. The ending is not killing him: he lets go, and breaks godhood so no one can ever rise that high again.

## The story on one page

What the player is told. A thousand years ago there were seven gods. The six betrayed the seventh, Isaren, whose people never had to live in fear of him. Before they could destroy him, he split his soul into fragments and scattered them across the land. Ever since, the six have hunted the fragments to stop him returning, and the world has been grey and hungry. A young apprentice in a hidden mountain village fuses with the main fragment, a voice with no memory that calls itself the Guide. When the Guide hears the old story, he decides he must have been the seventh god's disciple. He asks to be made whole, so the world can be prosperous again.

What the player does. They travel the six gods' lands to recover the fragments. In each region they meet someone unforgettable with a problem of their own, and help them solve it. That person helps them beat the region's boss and joins the journey. The six's minions and the gods themselves appear more and more often to stop the fragments coming together. Each fragment plays a memory from before the fall, and each region quietly teaches what the world used to be and how it broke.

What the player learns, slowly. The official history is a lie: there were seven gods. The seventh's people weren't abused or ruled by fear, and the six destroyed them rather than lose their own subjects, and with them their control. The Guide was never the disciple: he is Isaren himself. The places where fragments rest are tiny pockets of green in a dying world. The last fragment reveals why: the world's life is inside the fragments. In his final days Isaren drained the land to fuel his revenge and hid that stolen life inside his own shattered soul. The Withering, which everyone blames on the six or on fate, was him. How it ends. Made whole, Isaren is consumed by a thousand years of resentment. He means to save the world by ruling it: everyone else safe and treated fairly beneath him, and him alone on top to keep it that way. The companions, each of whom learned something on their own journey about ambition and standing over others, fight him and talk him down. Isaren doesn't die. He chooses to let go. He breaks the power that made him a god, so that no one can ever again become that powerful alone, and returns the life he hoarded to the land. The world begins to recover.

## The message

- To truly lead, you can't do it for your own ambition, and you can't put yourself above the people you're fighting for.

- Every companion's subplot carries a piece of it. Vera learns that surviving at others' expense is the same trap her lords set. Marcus learns that being "better than the slums" was the lie. Elena learns that rationed kindness is the church's game. Alex learns that rebellion for his own sake changes nothing. Jakob learns that a debt to the many can't be paid in private. Kira learns that believing whoever sounds sure is how the gods keep power.

- The six embody the obvious version: rigid hierarchy, the best elemental arms for those who enforce it, and rule by fear.

- Isaren embodies the hidden version, and only at the end. He hates what the six did to people, but has come to believe that only he can be trusted to stop it happening again. He wants to force fairness through control, with two tiers: everyone else, and him. This stays out of sight until the final act, so it lands as the twist rather than as a theme the player has been told all game.

## Tone

Character-driven, warm, often funny, with a sad history underneath. The player character stays a mostly blank self-insert (as in the original design), and the cast carries the personality. The memory cutscenes carry the dread.

## The world

## How power works

- Power comes from the world. The gods draw their might from the life of the land itself. They don't need anyone to believe in them; they need people to stay in their place.

- Control keeps it. The gods hold on to power through control and subjugation. They give the most powerful elemental items (weapons, armour, charms) to the officials, priests and generals who enforce their rule, and those people use and abuse them to keep everyone below them in place. Commoners get poor gear, and the poorest get none. This is how each region stays stratified, and why the gods need wars and slaughter to keep control.

- Every god has an element. Each god's power has an elemental base, so every item that god or their officials hand out carries that element. Each region's arms favour its god's fighting style (from the original design), which ties the weapons and elements docs into the world.

- The gods were mortals. Seven people grew so powerful there was no difference between them and gods. That fact matters for the ending: godhood is a thing that can be broken.

- Each region parallels a real country and its history, economy and problems (from the original design).

## Flourishing pockets: where the fragments are

Outside the fragment zones, the land is barely alive. Thin harvests, grey forests and angry, starving animals are normal; nobody alive remembers anything else. Every fragment rests in a small pocket that thrives: Haven, a sacred garden, an untouched valley, a spring that never freezes. People assume these places are blessed. The truth (the final memory) is that the world's life is pooled inside the fragments, and these pockets are what leaks out. Players should be able to notice this pattern themselves long before it is explained. The Guide even jokes about it ("Things grow wherever I've been. I must be very good company").

## The Withering

The name for the long death of the land. Each region has its own official story. The six say the land's health has to be paid for: heavy taxes keep the Withering back, and any region that stops paying will be taken by it. The Borderlands say it's the old war's curse, and the scholars say they're measuring it. Nobody connects it to the seventh god, because officially there was never a seventh god.

## The truth, in layers

The hook is a mystery told in small truths. Each layer answers the last question and raises a worse one. The player should always know a little more than the characters around them, and a little less than they want to.

## How the Guide changes (the slow tell)

The Guide is a companion in the player's head, and his personality drifts with each memory. This is the main trickle, done through voice rather than exposition.

- Fragments 1–2: curious, gentle, very funny. He can't taste, so he asks what everything tastes like. He apologises to doors before Soul Sight opens them.

- 3–4: protective of the player. He grows quietly angry whenever the six are mentioned. 5: knows who he is, and is proud and grieving. For the first time he calls the companions "my people".

- 6: bitter. He starts saying the six must "kneel" and "answer" for it.

- 7: defensive. "I protected the land's strength. Would you have left it for them?" 8: silent for a whole region. Then: "Bring me home. I'll make it right." He means it, and the player shouldn't be sure what "right" means.

## The cast

Every character gets one quirk you could do an impression of, one subplot the player solves, and one piece of the message they carry. Party of eight at the end: the player, Kira, Vera, Marcus, Elena, Jakob, Alex and Finn.

## The player

Finn's apprentice in Haven. A mostly blank self-insert, as in the original design: the cast supplies the colour, and the player supplies choices (sparing defeated enemies, how to answer each companion's crisis). The Guide lives in their head.

## The Guide (Isaren)

- Who: the voice of the fragment, with no memory at first. Once he hears the old story, he decides he must have been the seventh god's disciple, and says, a little sadly, that he'll never be as powerful as the great one. The player takes it as modesty; he means he'll never get his power back. Secretly he is the seventh god.

- Quirk: can't taste or touch, so he's obsessed with what things are like ("Describe the bread. No, properly"). He apologises to doors before Soul Sight opens them. After the tutorial he calls himself the seventh god's disciple, "though I'll never be what he was". Arc: see "How the Guide changes" above: warm, then proud, then bitter, then the final act.

## Master Finn, the blacksmith

- Who: Haven's old smith and the player's master. Secretly the last follower of Isaren, kept alive for a thousand years by the last of Isaren's power, and a legend nobles still tell stories about ("the Anvil of the Pass") without knowing he's real.

- Quirk: hums the same four notes while he works (the shrine door's chord, a clue). Claims his back is ruined, then catches a falling hammer without looking. Subplot: he set the player on the path on purpose. He's Isaren's friend, not his servant, repaying an old favour, and he has always known Isaren's last act was wrong. In the final arc he fights at the player's side and is the one who can tell Isaren the truth as a friend.

- Carries: loyalty that won't lie to you.

## Kira, the hunter's daughter

- Who: the player's childhood friend, an excellent archer, and food-motivated above all else.

- Quirk: recites confidently wrong "facts" from her Big Book of Facts, which she can't read ("Thornwolves can smell lies. That's why they always bite the liar first"). Rates every place by how good the food smells.

- Subplot, "The Book of Facts" (whole game, pays off at the Spire): in Noeth's city, where reading is licensed by rank, she secretly learns to read. Her precious book turns out to be a travelling priest's tithe ledger: years of her family's payments toward an elemental hunting bow that never came. She's crushed, then furious, then becomes the party's sceptic, the one who checks every claim. It's Kira who reads Noeth's records aloud and proves the Withering was Isaren.

- Carries: believing whoever sounds sure is how the gods keep power.

- Fights: bow. Nobody in Haven owns an elemental item, so her skill is entirely her own.

## Thomas, the hunter

Kira's dad, the source of most of her "facts". Kind, brave, gullible and proud of it. He trains the player in the tutorial. He has never owned an elemental item in his life, and he's the first proof that people without one can be very good.

## Vera, the bandit leader

- Who: leader of a small Borderlands crew, bandit and adventurer at once. She's sharp, funny, and exhausted.

- Quirk: describes her retirement cottage in more detail every time it comes up ("It has a blue door now. And a goat. The goat is called Pension"). Keeps a ledger of what everyone owes her: "You owe me a sandwich. From the ambush."

- Subplot, "The Last Job": she needs one big score to retire. The player helps steal the dungeon maps and clear the old war fortress. In return she gives up her necklace, which holds a fragment and has a twin worn by a high church official (paid off in Lumenvale). Along the way the player hears her crew's stories of how each of them was pushed into banditry. At the end she gets her treasure and buys the old homestead for the whole crew: the cottage becomes a farm everyone shares. Carries: surviving at others' expense is the trap the lords set.

- Fights: twin daggers and dirty tricks.

## Marcus, the slum kid who wants to be a knight

- Who: a gifted swordsman from Coinmere's drowned slums. Naive, hot-headed and arrogant.

- Quirk: practises his knightly title out loud ("Sir Marcus of the Lower Drain. Provisionally"). Keeps a running list of people who'll regret doubting him, and adds the player to it twice in the first hour.

- Subplot, "Sir Nobody" (Coinmere, then the Iron March):

- He and the player sneak into the upper city through the sewers. When they're caught, the guard captain is so impressed he takes Marcus as a squire, and Marcus leaves the party.

- Back in the slums, the player sees how kind and generous its people are, while Marcus quietly agrees with every guard who calls them thieves. He thinks it doesn't apply to him.

- Later, a jealous young noble, Lord Percival Vane, frames him for theft. At the same moment the nobles plan to have the slums "cleansed". The player clears his name. He's finally acknowledged, and turns the knighthood down to go home and

- protect the slums. They were the only people who were ever good to him.

- Carries: "I'm better than where I'm from" was the lie.

- Fights: sword. The best pure duellist in the party.

## Elena, the witch on the run

- Who: a former temple healer raising Pip, the orphaned child of an old friend. Quirk: threatens everyone with curses she can't cast ("Touch that and I'll turn your teeth into moths"). Names her herbs and scolds them when they wilt. Standoffish and quick to act, and fiercely soft with anyone who earns it.

- Subplot, "Sunpetal" (Lumenvale): Pip is sick, and the only cure is sunpetal, which grows only in Kerys's sacred garden. (It's a fragment zone, which is why anything grows there.) Elena ran from the church after healing a dying beggar with temple medicine reserved for the high-born. They called her a heretic and a traitor, and sentenced her to death. She recognises Finn's maker's mark on the player's weapon ("The Anvil of the Pass. My grandmother told stories about him. He was an enemy and an ally in a war nobody remembers"). At the end Pip is healed, and Elena heals pilgrims openly in the garden while the crowd stands between her and the Inquisition. Carries: kindness rationed by rank is the church's game.

- Fights: staff and herbs. The church took her temple staff, so she heals with herbs and a staff she carved herself.

## Jakob, the merchant who knows everything

- Who: a famous, impossible-to-find travelling merchant who deals in rare goods and rarer information. He's the game's fast travel.

- Quirk: is always already wherever you're going ("Took you long enough"). Trades secrets for secrets: "One for one. Fair's fair." Talks constantly, gossips shamelessly, and reads the player's past from tiny clues like a stage detective.

- Subplot, "A Name Worth Buying" (open-ended quests from Act 1, paying off in the Rimeholds): he's banned from the north and asks the player to retrieve his true name from Maioren's Hall of Lineage. The Spring fragment's memory shows the truth. A thousand years ago, young Jakob sold Isara's secret pass to Maioren in exchange for a lineage name for his family, and Maioren cursed him to live until the debt was paid. He has quietly guided fragment bearers ever since, and he was waiting for the player. The party argues about whether to forgive him, and the player decides. Either way he joins for good, and stops pretending he's only in it for profit.

- Carries: a debt to the many can't be paid in private.

- Fights: a cane that's secretly a long sword. He's far stronger than he lets on.

## Alex, the general's runaway son

- Who: the good-natured, rich do-gooder son of General Thorne. His late mother was a commoner barmaid, and he grew up in taverns listening to commoners' stories. Quirk: retells everything as a pub ballad, wildly embellished, and misattributes every quote ("As my mother always said, or possibly it was a goat..."). Carries his mother's old tankard everywhere and fights with it.

- Subplot, "The General's Son" (Iron March):

- He's run away in disgust at his father's brutality, and Thorne hires the player to bring him home. It's an escort mission, because Alex can barely fight.

- Mid-escort, a radical faction kidnaps him to use as leverage against his father. After the rescue, the player helps him see that running away and shouting changes nothing. He learns to rally commoners and make the case they can

- actually win: what the war costs everyone, and what the people being thrown away are worth.

- He stands up at his father's war council, where the player sees first-hand how lightly the generals spend lives. He stops the plan to march on Coinmere's slums, side by side with Marcus.

- Carries: rebellion for your own sake changes nothing.

- Fights: tankard and buckler. He starts weak and grows into the team's rallying support.

## General Thorne (not a companion)

Alex's father, and the Iron March's commander. Relatable and awful: he knows the slaughter is wrong, but he's "just a cog", so if he refuses, someone just as ruthless takes his place and his reward, so why not be the one who profits? He truly loves Alex and dismisses everything Alex cares about as youthful nonsense. He's the Iron March's main boss. He's beaten, not killed, and calls off the purge because of his son. He doesn't become a good man, only a slightly better one.

## Captain Bram Hollis (minor)

The sea captain, and the game's fast travel by water. Quirk: can't swim, and is wildly superstitious (he won't sail if a gull looks at him "funny"). His side quests are about the dockworkers and freight conditions in every port, and include an optional fight with the Tollkeeper, the harbour boss.

## The antagonists

- The six gods each appear in person in their region, rarely and terrifyingly. They were mortals once, and each still has a human flaw underneath the godhood: Agria's contempt for weakness, Ploutar's terror of having nothing, Kerys's need to be obeyed, Kratheon's love of order, Maioren's reverence for the past, Noeth's certainty that the clever deserve more.

- The Hollow Herald is the six's shared fragment-hunter and the recurring minion. It turns up in every region, a step behind or ahead of the player, and is finally fought at the Spire.

- Each region's champion (the main boss) is someone the region's god has blessed to keep the hierarchy in place.

## Plot outline, section by section

Every region follows the same shape: arrive → meet the companion and their problem → learn how this god keeps people down → the god appears → lead-up boss → main boss, beaten with the companion's help → fragment and memory → the companion's subplot resolves. The order of regions is fixed for the story; side content stays open. Rough hours are in brackets.

## Prologue: Haven (hours 0–3)

- Companion: Kira. Bosses: the Warden (in the shrine), then Master Finn (a sparring test).

- Finn sends his apprentice to fetch "some old metalwork" from the shrine deep in Whisperwood. Kira tags along, and Thomas teaches them to fight.

- In the shrine a trap separates the two, and the player fuses with the Heart. The Guide wakes with no memory, senses another fragment, and leads the player to the guardian's room. The player is reunited with Kira, and together they beat the Warden. They find the Ember and the item Finn wanted.

- Back home, Finn notices something different and tests the player in a sparring match, holding back so obviously that it's funny. He hands over a real weapon, says he "dabbled in fighting when he was young", and laughs it off as an old man's dreams. World lesson: the voice says the world outside is dying, and Haven is the only green place anyone in the village has ever seen. (The full scene-by-scene script is in its own doc, Seven Gods RPG — First Arc Script.)

Act 1, "The Road Out"

1. The Borderlands, Agria's frontier (hours 3–8)

- Companion: Vera, "The Last Job". Lead-up: Hask the Gatebreaker. Main boss: Warlord Grimm, Agria's blessed warlord (Iron Colossus template).

- The player's first real fight is against Vera's crew, and the player wins alone. Vera begs for help raiding the old war fortress, offering her necklace as payment, and the Guide goes very quiet when he sees it.

- Stealing the dungeon maps from a rival crew, escorting her people, and hearing each bandit's story of how they ended up here.

- Grimm's crew is squeezing everyone smaller. Agria's doctrine is that the strong eat, and her best arms go to the cruelest.

- God encounter, Agria's Hunt: Agria's blessed run down the weak for sport while she watches. She smells the fragment on the player and lets them go out of contempt: "Come back when you're worth eating."

- Fragment: Vera's necklace (memory 3: the migration, and Agria demanding her people back). Vera learns the necklace was stolen from a church official, and that it had a twin.

- Resolution: Vera gets her treasure and buys the homestead for the whole crew, then joins the player "until the goat's paid for".

- World lesson: the Borderlands were green once. This is where the old war started.

2. Coinmere, Ploutar's gilded capital (hours 8–13)

- Companion: Marcus, "Sir Nobody" part 1. Lead-up: the Bailiff (collecting a debt from Marcus's grandmother). Main boss: the Ledger Kraken in Lord Chancellor

- Blackwood's flooded vault under the upper city.

- Stopped at the gate with Marcus, the player sneaks in through the sewers with him. That's the dungeon, and the sewers run into Blackwood's vault.

- In the slums the player sees a community that shares everything, while Marcus agrees with every guard who calls them thieves.

- Jakob properly enters here: the first of his open-ended secret-for-secret quests points at the vault.

- God encounter, Ploutar's Golden Auction: Ploutar's gold-element arms and charms are auctioned to nobles. The player watches people's worth decided by price, and stands next to a commoner who's just been outbid for the healing charm her child needs.

- Fragment: the Vault fragment (memory 4: the six's secret council, and "in ten years we'll rule empty fields"). This is the first proof the official history was rewritten on purpose.

- Resolution (for now): caught at the end, the guard captain takes Marcus as a squire. He leaves the party, delighted, and promises to knight the player "when I'm in charge".

Act 2, "Whose Gods?"

3. Lumenvale, Kerys's cathedral lands (hours 13–18)

- Companion: Elena, "Sunpetal". Lead-up: Brother Ashlock, the Purifier. Main boss: High Inquisitor Mordecai and his summoned Herald (Hymnal Roc template).

- The player meets Elena in a village where she heals people in secret. She recognises Finn's mark, threatens the player with moth-teeth, and eventually lets them help. Pip needs sunpetal, which grows only in the sacred garden, a fragment zone. Healing here is rationed by rank, and Elena's "crime" was ignoring that.

- Mordecai wears the twin of Vera's necklace, and Vera comes along for that one. God encounter, Kerys's Heretic's Trial: Elena is captured and tried in front of the whole cathedral. Kerys speaks through every stained-glass window. The player and party break the trial up, and the crowd's reaction starts to turn.

- Fragment: the Garden fragment (memory 5). The Guide remembers his name: Isaren. The midpoint reveal: the player has been carrying the seventh god.

- Resolution: Pip is healed and sent to Haven (Thomas "adopts" the kid instantly). Elena heals pilgrims openly in the garden while the crowd stands between her and the Inquisition, then joins the party.

4. The Iron March, Kratheon's war provinces (hours 18–24)

- Companion: Alex, "The General's Son", plus Marcus, "Sir Nobody" part 2. Lead-up: Drum-Major Varro. Main boss: General Thorne's War Wagon.

- General Thorne hires the player to bring his runaway son home. The escort goes badly, Alex is kidnapped by radicals, and the player rescues him.

- Alex learns to rally commoners rather than just run.

- The war council: the player sits in on Thorne's war council and watches generals spend lives like coins. Coinmere's nobles have hired Kratheon's army to "cleanse" the slums before they rise up.

- Meanwhile Marcus has been framed for theft by Lord Percival Vane and thrown out of the knights. The player clears his name (a duel, plus proof dug out of Vane's household). Marcus is acknowledged, then turns the knighthood down to defend the slums, and rejoins for good.

- God encounter, Kratheon's Parade: the god reviews his troops, and executes a deserter by simply ordering him to stop breathing. The Hollow Herald reports the fragment to him.

- Fragment: the Valley fragment, from a peaceful valley both armies are too superstitious to enter (memory 6). Young Finn begs Isaren to flee, and Haven is founded by his refugees. Finn, over the communication crystal: "Family resemblance. Great-great-something grandfather. Lovely man."

- Resolution: with Alex standing at the front of the commoners and Marcus at the slum gate, the march on Coinmere is broken. Thorne calls it off. Alex joins.

Act 3, "The Cost"

5. The Rimeholds, Maioren's north (hours 24–29)

- Companion: Jakob, "A Name Worth Buying". Lead-up: the Twin Proctors, keepers of the Hall of Lineage. Main boss: the Frost Conclave, five elders bound as one (Chorus of Five template).

- In the north your bloodline decides your rank, your job and even your name. Jakob, banned, asks the player to retrieve his true name from the Hall of Lineage. God encounter, the Festival of Remembrance: every family's ancestors are read aloud, and people with no lineage aren't allowed to mourn their dead.

- Fragment: the Spring fragment, from a spring that never freezes (memory 7). Jakob's betrayal a thousand years ago, then Isaren draining his own fields.

- Resolution: the party argues about Jakob, and the player decides. Jakob joins openly. The Guide insists he was protecting the land, and the party starts to notice how defensive he sounds.

6. Noeth's Spire, the scholar city (hours 29–34)

- Kira's subplot pays off, "The Book of Facts". Lead-up: the Hollow Herald, finally cornered. Main boss: the Hundred-Eyed Archive, Noeth's living library.

- In Noeth's city, reading is licensed by rank. Kira learns to read in secret and discovers what her Book of Facts really is.

- God encounter, Noeth's Examination: the citizens are tested, and the unblessed are barred from the libraries for life.

- Fragment: the Eye fragment (memory 8). The Withering was Isaren. The Guide falls silent for the rest of the region.

- Kira reads Noeth's sealed records aloud, and they confirm it: the instruments, the dates, and the flow of the world's life into Isara.

- Resolution: the party now carries every fragment, and the life of the world. The Guide's only words: "Bring me home. I'll make it right."

## Act 4, "The Gathering" (hours 34–40)

- The six come in person. With every fragment in one place, the gods stop sending minions and march, together, for the first time in a thousand years. This is the original design's all-out war.

- The hidden allies rise: Haven, the fragment-pocket communities, and everyone the companions helped along the way (Vera's crew, the slum families, Elena's pilgrims, Alex's commoners).

- The god fights (8 against 1): each god is fought with the companion from their region at the heart of the plan. Each victory breaks a god rather than killing them: their hold on their people collapses and they're left diminished. Some of them try to warn the player. Noeth: "We were wrong about why we fought him. Not that we did."

- Master Finn reveals himself, the Anvil of the Pass, and fights as the eighth member of the party.

- The Pantheon Gate, then the Warden, Last Watch, guarding the road into the Ashen Heart. It remembers the player, and hesitates.

- The Ashen Heart: see the next section.

## The ending

## Homecoming

The party carries the fragments into the grey ruins of Isara and sets them on the empty seat where Isaren once sat among his people. For a moment it's everything the player hoped for. The Guide becomes Isaren, whole and solid, and green bursts out across the ruin for a hundred paces. Then it stops. He's holding it.

## The two tiers

This is the first time the game states Isaren's philosophy outright:

- "No lords. No priests. No generals. No one hoarding the best arms to keep everyone else afraid, no one born to rule. None of them afraid, ever again. And me above them, so no one can ever do to them what the six did to mine. Isn't that what you fought for?"

He means it. He's spent a thousand years in pieces, and his love for his people has curdled into the certainty that only he can be trusted. Kira holds up Noeth's records: the world is dying because he's holding its life. He knows. "It waited a thousand years. It can wait until the six are in chains."

Each companion answers him with what their own journey taught them. Vera's homestead, Marcus turning down the knighthood, Elena healing in the open, Alex in front of the commoners, Jakob's debt, Kira's book. Each one is a version of you can't lead people from above them. He doesn't listen. The fight begins.

## The final battle

In the boss doc this is four phases:

| 1. | The Heart and the eight shards. He splits his soul again, mockingly, into one shard bound to each fighter. These are the eight fragments the party carried. |
| --- | --- |
| 2. | The mirror team. "Why do you need each other? I can be all of you." His shards become clones of the party, and the party is weakened because he has taken their strength back. |
| 3. | The last fighter. He sheds godhood's grandeur and fights as the mortal he was before: the greatest fighter of his age, alone. |
| 4. | The two tiers. Enthroned, he offers protection to anyone who will stand beneath him, and asks for one fighter to be sacrificed "for the good of all". The party wins by refusing both together. |

## The letting go

When his last phase breaks, Isaren isn't dead, just beaten and finally listening. Master Finn steps forward, not as a follower but as the friend who left with the refugees a thousand years ago: "You told me once the reason you'd never sit higher than anyone was that power that high doesn't belong to a person. Did you forget, or did you stop believing it?"

The player has the last word, a fixed scene with no choice: everything they watched the companions learn, said back to him. Isaren looks at the grey world and at the green pocket around his feet. Then he chooses:

- He breaks the power that made him a god, the bond that lets one person draw the world's life into themselves. He breaks it for good, so that no one, not him and not anyone after him, can ever again become that powerful alone.

- Because godhood itself is broken, the six lose their divinity too. They can no longer draw on the land, and the elemental arms they handed out lose their edge. He releases the life he hoarded back into the land. The green doesn't arrive all at once. It starts at Haven, the garden, the valley and the spring, every pocket where a fragment rested, and spreads outward.

- His last line, to the player, sounding like the Guide again: "Tell me what the bread tastes like. Properly, this time." Then he's gone, dispersed into everything.

## Epilogue

Short scenes, each shaped by the player's choices:

- Vera's homestead has a blue door and a goat called Pension, and it's crowded. Marcus runs a sword school in the drowned slums, for free. Lord Vane is a student, grudgingly.

- Elena opens a clinic in Lumenvale. Pip insists on being called "Head Healer". Alex speaks for the commoners in a council his father now has to answer to. Thorne attends, and argues, and listens slightly more each time.

- Jakob finally stops ageing, and starts selling secrets at half price.

- Kira writes a new Big Book of Facts. Every fact has a source.

- Finn goes back to his forge in Haven and finally lets himself be old.

- The six, now mortal: some face trial, some vanish, and Agria is last seen failing to grow turnips.

This answers the question in the original design ("what are the implications of beating the religions?"): the churches lose their divine backing, and kingdoms have to govern without gods. It isn't a utopia, just a chance, and a world green enough to have one.

## Open questions

- The god names. Do Isaren, Agria, Ploutar, Kerys, Kratheon, Maioren and Noeth work for you? Alternatives for the head god if Isaren is too close: Aequor (Latin, level, like a calm sea) or Isonne.

- "Sent to rule for 10,000 years." The original design says the seven were sent to the continent. This outline keeps them as mortals who rose, and leaves sent by whom unanswered. It could be a post-game or sequel hook, or cut.

- The six after the ending. Broken and mortal is the default. Should any of them get a redemption scene, or should they stay antagonists to the end?

- Jakob's betrayal. Should forgiving him (or not) change anything mechanically, such as his fast-travel prices or the epilogue?

- Region order. Lumenvale and the Iron March could swap, but the midpoint reveal (the Guide is Isaren) should stay roughly halfway.

- Docs to update next: the Elements doc and Enemy doc still use the old god names and regions (Trade Routes, Youth Academy). The boss doc has been updated to match this outline.
